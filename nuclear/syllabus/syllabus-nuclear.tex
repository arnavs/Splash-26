\subsection*{Course Overview} Humanity's childhood as a species ended with its first nuclear explosion. More than any other, these devices represent us: our delight in our "new [physical] powers," and our "Promethean guilt" at the recognition that these powers would inevitably turn to violence, as "all our ways are rooted in it."\footnote{To quote \href{http://large.stanford.edu/courses/2016/ph241/anderson1/}{\tt J.R. Oppenheimer}.}

The twelve thousand or so nuclear weapons strewn about the planet are doing things, even while dormant. But what are they doing? What physical, moral and strategic principles undergird their operation? What's the difference between a thermonuclear device and a pure fission weapon; is there truly such a thing as a "tactical nuke,"; is the US nuclear launch authority sensible; did the NPT and CTBT make us safer; and what's the point of the nuclear triad? How were these things made, and how could they be unmade?

This class is an attempt to explore these questions. One third of the time will be spent on the physics, and the rest will be spent on the game theory and strategic concerns.

We will not answer all of these questions, but we all live within range of a nuclear ICBM, and you will leave with a much better understanding of them.
