    ### Claude's proposed outline for Section 3 (~60 min)

    - The power to hurt (3 min)
        - Schelling: "The power to hurt is bargaining power. To exploit it is diplomacy—vicious diplomacy, but diplomacy." (schelling01)
    - MAD as a game tree (12 min)
        - The Soviets choose attack or not; then the US chooses retaliate or not.
        - Backward induction: once your cities are gone, retaliating saves nothing, so the threat isn't credible.
        - The class votes at each node before the answer is revealed.
        - Puzzle for the rest of the hour: how do you make a non-credible threat credible?
    - Way out 1: take away your own choice (commitment) (10 min)
        - Prune the "don't retaliate" branch from the tree.
        - Berlin garrison as a tripwire: "Bluntly, they can die." (schelling02)
        - Doomsday machine: Strangelove clip ("the whole point... is lost if you keep it a secret"); the real Soviet Perimeter / "Dead Hand"
        - Pre-delegation: Davy Crockett callback; who has the authority to fire?
    - Way out 2: make sure you can still answer (survivability) (10 min)
        - If a first strike can destroy your missiles, your branch of the tree disappears.
        - Callback to the triad classification: this is why submarines matter.
        - Interactive sim: a first-strike calculator (silos, warheads per missile, accuracy) showing how MIRV makes striking first pay
    - Way out 3: the threat that leaves something to chance (brinkmanship) (12 min)
        - Schelling's two climbers tied together on a crumbling edge (schelling03)
        - Live game: two volunteers play chicken with a die; each round both stand firm, the risk of "war" goes up by one face
        - Cuban Missile Crisis: submarine B-59 and Vasili Arkhipov
    - When it almost went wrong: 1983 (6 min)
        - The KGB's RYAN tasking (syllabus doc), Able Archer 83, Stanislav Petrov's false alarm
        - Is sole presidential launch authority sensible?
    - Did arms control work? (5 min)
        - Real charts: nuclear tests per year, warheads over time (peak ~70,000 in 1986)
        - Sakharov and the 1963 test ban; NPT: 9 nuclear states instead of the ~20 Kennedy feared
    - Ending (2 min)
        - The WIPP marker message: "This place is a message... This place is not a place of honor... The danger is still present, in your time, as it was in ours." (wipp.pdf, App. F)
