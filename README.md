### H16581 Archaeogaming: A Site Visit to Skyrim

> The snows of Skyrim fall on over 4000 years of history. They cover the first footprints of Ysgramor, the brooding dungeons of the departed Dwemer, the forbidding temples of the Dragon Cult, and the still-sodden battlefields of the Empire and the Stormcloaks.
>
> This class will be an interactive exploration of a small part of this history. We will inspect an ancient Nordic barrow, starting with the exterior surface and proceeding through deeper stratigraphy.
> 
> Throughout, we will engage the basic questions of all archaeology: who built this, and for whom? What is this place intended to convey, and how is that reflected in its contents and design? And what does the state of the site tell us about what has happened to it since?

### B16582 The Lord of the Rings as Elegy: A Brief History of Arda Marred

> "For this is the Ring of Fire, and with it you may rekindle hearts in a world that grows chill." Cirdan said this to Gandalf upon his disembarking in Mithlond. But his statement referred to more than the looming War of the Ring.
>
> In fact, the world of the Legendarium (also known as Arda) is continually in the process of "growing chill," as entropy wears away the primordial integrity of its creation. The great kingdom of Numenor fell into decay (first moral, then physical) and now persists only as "a memory brought over the hills." The Phial of Galadriel is but "an echo of an echo" of the original Light of Valinor. And even Mordor -- "that vast fortress... secure in its pride and its immeasurable strength" -- is itself a paltry imitation of Morgoth's stronghold of Utumno.
>
> This class will be an interactive seminar on decay and wearying in Arda, and the concomitant grief (elegy) of those who witness it. We will examine how these elements are woven into the text, from Bilbo's farewell in Chapter 1 to the last line of the final appendix. And how the "ever-mounting burden of years" bears upon the tale.
>
> The class will be a mix of discussion, solo contemplation and writing, and potentially peer-to-peer feedback.

### X16580 Nuclear Weapons: Theory and Practice

> Humanity's childhood as a species ended with its first nuclear explosion. More than any other, these devices represent us: our delight in our "new [physical] powers," and our "Promethean guilt" at the recognition that these powers would inevitably turn to violence, as "all our ways are rooted in it."
> 
> The twelve thousand or so nuclear weapons strewn about the planet are doing things, even while dormant. But what are they doing? What physical, moral and strategic principles undergird their operation? What's the difference between a thermonuclear device and a pure fission weapon; is there truly such a thing as a "tactical nuke,"; is the US nuclear launch authority sensible; did the NPT and CTBT make us safer; and what's the point of the nuclear triad? How were these things made, and how could they be unmade?
> 
> This class is an attempt to explore these questions. One third of the time will be spent on the physics, and the rest will be spent on the game theory and strategic concerns.
> 
> We will not answer all of these questions, but we all live within range of a nuclear ICBM, and you will leave with a much better understanding of them.
