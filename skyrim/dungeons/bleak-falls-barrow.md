**The three questions, at every stop:**
1. **Who** built this, and **for whom**?
2. **What** is it meant to convey, and how does the design show it?
3. **What has happened** to it since?

### Verbal asides (not on the slides)
- **Dates.** `1E 700` means year 700 of the First Era. Merethic dates count **down** to 1E 0, so ME 1000 is older than ME 1.
- **Spans.** 1E 0 to 4E 201 is 2,920 + 896 + 433 + 201 = **4,450 years**. Anything Merethic (Ysgramor, the Dragon Cult, these barrows) is older than that, but by an unknown amount.
- **Alduin.** Alduin is the World-Eater, the first dragon. He couldn't be killed, so at the end of the Dragon War the Nord Tongues used an Elder Scroll on the Throat of the World to cast him *out of time*. In 4E 201 he's back, raising dragons from their burial mounds. That's why the Dragonstone at the bottom of this barrow matters.

## Before class

- **Save below level 18.** The site is levelled (range 6–20, capped at 20). At level 18+ a frost troll and the body of a Breton climber (Thomas) appear, and the boss scales from a Draugr Overlord to a Dragon Priest. A low-level save keeps the route predictable. (The fact that the site's contents depend on who's looking is a teaching point of its own; see below.)
- Make saves at the entrance and at the start of each zone, so you can skip ahead if time runs short.
- On PC, `tgm` (god mode) and `tcl` (noclip) keep combat from eating class time. Turn up the brightness for the projector.
- Take backup screenshots of each stop in case the game or projector fails.
- Have the read-alouds ready: Arvel's journal, the Golden Claw's palm, the Word Wall translation, the Dragonstone inscription.

## The route

UESP divides the site into the **exterior**, **Bleak Falls Temple** (upper zone) and **Bleak Falls Sanctum** (lower zone).

### Stop 0 — Riverwood: the object before the site
- **What happens:** Lucan Valerius's shop has been robbed of one thing: *"An ornament, solid gold. In the shape of a dragon's claw."*
- **Provenance:** his sister Camilla: *"Lucan found the claw about a year after he opened the store. He never quite explained where he got it. He's a tricky one."*
- **Ask:** We meet the artifact *before* the site, as a shop decoration with no history attached. How often is that true of real museum objects?
- **Note:** UESP's lore page calls the Claw a Valerius family heirloom, but nothing in Lucan's dialogue supports that, and Camilla contradicts it. Treat the heirloom story as **(unverified)**.

### Stop 1 — The approach and exterior
- **What you see:** monumental arches on a mountainside above Riverwood, and an old watchtower (Camilla: *"You'll know you're in the right place once you spot the old watchtower"*). Bandits hold both the tower and the barrow entrance.
- **Ask (Q1/Q2):** Why build *here*, high, visible, over the road? Who was meant to see it?
- **Ask (Q3):** The bandits are using a 4,000-year-old tomb as a fort. Reuse starts before you go in.

### Stop 2 — Entry hall: the squatters' layer
- **What you see:** a campfire, bedrolls, dead skeevers, two dead bandits, and two live ones arguing about their partner:
  > "So we're just supposed to sit here while Arvel runs off with that golden claw?"
  > "That dark elf wants to go on ahead, let him. Better than us risking our necks."
- **Toolkit (terminus post quem and sealing):** bandit coins and gear date the bandits, not the barrow. This is an **open floor**, so the newest object only tells you the last visitor was here. Nothing seals it.

### Stop 3 — The pillar puzzle: a lock for readers
- **What you see:** three rotating pillars with animal glyphs, a gate, and a lever. A bandit pulls the lever and is shot by the dart trap.
- **The clue:** three statues, *"two of which are found above the gate. The third (which is in fact the middle statue) has fallen to the floor next to the lever."*
- **Solution:** **Snake, Snake, Whale**, starting from the pillar farthest from the gate.
- **Ask (Q2):** The answer is on the wall. So who is this lock meant to stop? People who can't read the iconography. The bandit dies of illiteracy.
- **Ask (Q3):** One clue has *fallen off*. Decay changes how readable a site is, and the bandit may have been working from a broken text.

### Stop 4 — Spiders and Arvel the Swift
- **What you see:** a web-filled chamber with a frostbite spider, egg sacs and four desiccated corpses. Arvel is stuck in the web:
  > "Yes, the claw. I know how it works. The claw, the markings, the door in the Hall of Stories. I know how they all fit together! Help me down, and I'll show you. You won't believe the power the Nords have hidden there."
- **Freed, he runs:** *"You fool! Why should I share the treasure with anyone?"* He dies further in (draugr or a spike-wall trap). His body carries the Claw and his journal.
- **Read aloud: Arvel's Journal (complete):**
  > My fingers are trembling. The Golden Claw is finally in my hands, and with it, the power of the ancient Nordic heroes. That fool Lucan Valerius had no idea that his favorite store decoration was actually the key to Bleak Falls Barrow.
  > Now I just need to get to the Hall of Stories and unlock the door. The legend says there is a test that the Nords put in place to keep the unworthy away, but that "When you have the golden claw, the solution is in the palm of your hands."
- **Teaching points:**
  - The journal is a **primary source written by a looter**. It's useful but self-serving: he's sure it's about "power".
  - The spiders and skeevers are **bioturbation**: animals living in a site and mixing up what's in it.

### Stop 5 — Embalming room and catacombs: the original deposit
- **What you see:** *"a complete set of embalming tools"*, burial urns, and *"corpses lining the walls"*. Only the bodies in armour (male) or tattered clothes (female) get up as draugr. Those still in wraps, or naked, are *"permanently dead"*.
- **Ask (Q1):** The embalming room shows the dead were *processed* here. Who did that work?
- **Ask:** Some of the dead are armed and some are wrapped. What does the difference in treatment suggest? Guardians and ordinary dead? A hierarchy?
- **Ask:** Draugr wake when you come in. Is the tomb keeping intruders **out**, or the dead **in**?
- **The coin problem (key archaeogaming point):** draugr and urns here hold gold, and UESP notes that *"Although officially the coins are called Septims"*. Taken literally, a septim in a sealed Merethic urn gives the burial a terminus post quem in the Third Era, which is absurd. The real explanation is a **game-design concession**: the player needs loot. The designers are a formation process too. Knowing *how* a deposit was made comes before dating anything in it.

### Stop 6 — Through to the Sanctum
- **What you see:** swinging blades, an oil slick, a waterfall cavern where a draugr breaks out of a sarcophagus, and a final, stronger draugr at the Sanctum doors.
- **Level-scaled evidence:** at level 18+ there is also a frost troll, and the body of Thomas, *"a Breton who evidently died trying to climb down the rope"*. That corpse only exists for some players.
- **Ask:** If a site's evidence depends on who is looking, what does that do to reproducibility? (This one only happens in games, but it echoes real observer effects.)

### Stop 7 — The claw door in the Hall of Stories
- **What you see:** a door with *"three concentric circles… marked with animal symbols"* and a claw-shaped keyhole.
- **Solution:** turn the Claw over. From outer ring to inner: **Bear, Moth, Owl**. Then insert the Claw.
- **Teaching point:** the key and the lock were **made together as one system**. The skill that opens the door is literally reading the object: turning it over and looking at the palm.
- **Ask:** The Claw got *out* of the barrow somehow and ended up in a shop. UESP doesn't say how **(unverified)**. Has the Sanctum really been sealed all this time?

### Stop 8 — The Sanctum: Word Wall, Guardian, Dragonstone
- **Word Wall** (read the translation aloud):
  > HET NOK FAAL VAHLOK / DEINMAAR DO DOVAHGOLZ / AHRK AAN FUS DO UNSLAAD / RAHGOL AHRK VULOM
  > *"Here lies The Guardian / keeper of the Dragonstone / and a force of eternal / rage and darkness"*

  You learn **FUS** ("force"), the first word of Unrelenting Force.
- **The Guardian:** a Draugr Overlord at low levels (a Dragon Priest at higher levels). It carries the **Dragonstone**: a map of dragon burial sites on one side and dragon script on the other. (UESP isn't consistent about which side is which.) The script reads:
  > "Here lie our / Fallen lords / until power of / ALDUIN restore"
- **Teaching points:**
  - **Text tells you the room's purpose.** The wall says outright that this is a guardian's tomb, and that the guardian keeps the stone.
  - **Association:** if the Sanctum is a sealed context, the Wall, the Guardian and the Stone belong to one event. UESP's lore says the Dragonstone was *"crafted following the Dragon War… in the days that followed Alduin's defeat."* That makes the Stone a terminus post quem for the Sanctum: it was sealed *after* the Dragon War.
  - **Q2 payoff:** the deepest, best-protected thing here is a **map** and a **prayer**. These are the Dragon Cult as historical losers, recording where their gods are buried and waiting for Alduin to come back. The tomb is a waiting room.
- **Exit:** a lever lowers a rock wall onto *"a pedestal with a human skull and flower offerings (two blue, two red, two purple mountain flowers)"*. A back exit opens onto the far side of the mountain.
- **Ask (Q3):** Fresh flowers by a skull, behind a sealed door. Who left them, and when? We can't tell. Is it evidence of continued ritual, or set dressing?

### Stop 9 — After the dig: the find goes to an institution
- **Farengar** (the Jarl's court wizard) wanted *"a 'Dragonstone,' said to contain a map of dragon burial sites"*. On hand-in he says: *"My... associate here will be pleased… She discovered its location, by means she has so far declined to share with me."*
- **Delphine** later: *"I arranged to have Farengar recover the dragonstone for me."* She copied the map by **rubbings**. Her *Map of Dragon Burials* *"is less accurate than the original stone (for example, the mound near Dawnstar is missing)."*
- **Ask:** The artifact passes from tomb to court wizard to a secret order, and the copy loses data. Compare real debates about who should hold finds and what reproductions lose.

## Using the toolkit here (and where it breaks)

| Principle | In Bleak Falls |
|-----------|----------------|
| Superposition | **Doesn't really apply.** The barrow was built in one go. The Sanctum is deeper because it's *protected*, not because it's older. Bandits are at the front because it's *accessible*. Worth saying out loud after the *4,450+ years, stacked* slide: "depth" here means access, not time. |
| Original horizontality | No real test here: these are built rooms, not laid-down deposits. |
| Association | The Sanctum, if it was sealed: Word Wall + Guardian + Dragonstone. |
| Terminus post quem | Bandit coins (open floor: they only date the last visitor). Dragonstone (made after the Dragon War, so the Sanctum was sealed after it). Draugr septims (a game artifact, not evidence). |
| Terminus ante quem | Weak here. You could treat the cave-in, or the rock wall at the exit, as a seal, but nothing in the game dates it. |
| Disturbance | Skeevers, spiders, bandits, Arvel, *you*. |

## Site biography (build on the board)

1. Built by Dragon Cult Nords in the Merethic Era: a tomb, and a repository for dragon knowledge.
2. After the Dragon War (Alduin's defeat), the Sanctum is fitted out: the Guardian, the Dragonstone and the Word Wall. Then it is sealed behind the claw door.
3. Thousands of years of abandonment. Skeevers and spiders move in, a clue statue falls, the bodies desiccate.
4. The Golden Claw leaves the site somehow, and Lucan "finds" it. *(How is unknown.)*
5. Arvel steals the Claw back and dies trying to use it. Bandits occupy the upper halls.
6. *You* arrive, as another disturbance. The Dragonstone goes to Farengar, then to Delphine as a rubbing.
